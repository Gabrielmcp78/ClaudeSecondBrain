# Shakespearience Fiscal Sponsorship: Vetting Checklist & Candidates

This report outlines the operational vetting checklist and candidate profiles for securing a Model C fiscal sponsor for Shakespearience. Model C fiscal sponsorship allows Shakespearience to function as a separate commercial entity while utilizing a tax-exempt sponsor as a conduit to receive grants and tax-deductible donations.

---

## 1. Operational Vetting Checklist

Every prospective fiscal sponsor must be evaluated against these six criteria to protect Shakespearience's intellectual property, operational speed, and financial resources.

### IRS Discretion and Control
The contract must explicitly state that the sponsor maintains legal discretion and control over the funds. This is a non-negotiable IRS requirement for Model C fiscal sponsorship. Without this clause, the IRS does not recognize the arrangement, and individual donations will not be tax-deductible.

### Administrative Fee Structure
Sponsor fees typically range from 5% to 10% of regranted funds. Evaluate:
- Whether the fee is flat or tiered (e.g., decreasing percentages for larger grant volumes).
- If the fee applies to all revenue categories (individual donations, government grants, corporate sponsorships) or only specific streams.
- The presence of hidden costs such as initial setup fees, monthly account maintenance fees, or transaction fees.

### Disbursal Speed and Milestone Schedules
Model C relationships function on either a reimbursement basis or milestone-based advances. Establish:
- The exact timeline for fund release after the sponsor receives a grant (e.g., net-15 or net-30 days).
- The administrative burden required to trigger disbursals, including invoice submittals, progress reports, or expense receipts.
- Whether the sponsor permits advances for initial project expenses or requires Shakespearience to float costs.

### Intellectual Property Preservation
The agreement must explicitly state that Shakespearience retains 100% ownership of all intellectual property, including source code, audio assets, script rights, characters, and trademarks. The sponsor acts strictly as a financial conduit and has no claim to project assets.

### Liability and Indemnification
The contract must define clear boundaries of liability. Shakespearience must indemnify the sponsor against operational liabilities arising from Shakespearience's activities. Conversely, the sponsor must ensure that liabilities from other sponsored projects under their umbrella do not expose Shakespearience's assets.

### Termination and Asset Transfer
The agreement must specify termination terms. If Shakespearience secures its own 501(c)(3) status or changes fiscal sponsors, any remaining funds earmarked for the project must be transferable to the new tax-exempt entity. Ensure the contract does not contain clauses that forfeit unused funds to the sponsor upon termination.

## 2. Fiscal Sponsor Candidates

The following five organizations support media, arts, and educational projects nationally or regionally in Texas.

### Fractured Atlas
- **Focus**: Arts, media, and performing arts.
- **Application Process**: Open, rolling online application; fast turnaround (typically 1–2 weeks).
- **Administrative Fee**: 8% on all incoming funds.
- **Relevance**: Highly reliable national platform with an established donor-management portal, automated tax receipting, and extensive experience supporting digital arts and media projects.

### Independent Arts & Media (IAM)
- **Focus**: Independent media, podcasts, audio drama, and digital culture.
- **Application Process**: Quarterly application cycles with a steering committee review.
- **Administrative Fee**: 7% to 10% depending on the project's projected budget.
- **Relevance**: Direct alignment with the audio-first, educational, and digital distribution model of Shakespearience. Excellent fit for projects crossing journalism, performance, and technology.

### The Gotham (formerly Independent Features Project)
- **Focus**: Storytellers, filmmakers, and audio creators.
- **Application Process**: Selective application process; requires proof of narrative and artistic quality.
- **Administrative Fee**: 7% to 8%.
- **Relevance**: Specifically designed for audio drama, podcast, and cinematic storytelling initiatives. Best choice if the project targets large-scale media grants or film-industry foundation support.

### Austin Film Society (AFS)
- **Focus**: Texas-based filmmakers and digital media creators.
- **Application Process**: Open applications for Texas residents or projects with strong regional ties.
- **Administrative Fee**: Typically 7%.
- **Relevance**: A local Texas sponsor. Gabriel's Martindale residency satisfies the regional requirement. AFS provides access to Texas-specific grants, local corporate sponsors, and regional donor networks.

### The Field
- **Focus**: Performing arts, independent artists, and cross-disciplinary performance projects.
- **Application Process**: Rolling membership model; low barrier to entry.
- **Administrative Fee**: 7% for active members.
- **Relevance**: Fits the theatrical, performance, and classical education components of the Shakespearience mission.
