# Playbook 10 — Insurance & Risk

*The coverage a family-media company actually needs — enough to sleep at night, not so much you're insuring against ghosts. For an audio-content business serving children, a few specific policies matter more than the generic list.*

**Audience:** Gabriel (owner decision); confirm specifics with a broker.
**Last updated:** 2026-07-13
**Not insurance advice** — get real quotes; a small-business broker (Insureon, Next, Hiscox, The Hartford) will right-size this in an afternoon.

---

## What actually applies to you

- **General Liability (GL)** — the baseline. Third-party bodily injury / property damage. Low risk for a remote audio business, but it's cheap, often required for live events, and the foundation of a Business Owner's Policy. ~$45–$123/mo for a small business (see Ops Operating Costs).
- **Professional Liability / Errors & Omissions (E&O)** — covers claims that your service or content caused harm (bad advice, a mistake in curriculum a school relied on). Relevant because you sell educational product to schools.
- **Media Liability** — the one people miss and the one most on-point here: covers defamation, copyright/IP infringement, and rights claims arising from *published content*. A Shakespeare-adaptation podcast publishing scripts, music, and artwork is exactly the risk profile this exists for. Often bundled with E&O as "Media/Tech E&O."
- **Cyber Liability** — covers a data breach: notification costs, response, liability. It matters more because you hold children's and school data (ties to COPPA, Playbook 05). Worth a quote once real member data exists.

## What to skip (for now)

- **Workers' comp** — not needed until you have W-2 employees; contractors don't trigger it (Playbook 08).
- **Commercial property / auto** — no premises or vehicles in the business.
- **D&O** — revisit only if you take outside investment or add a formal board.

## The efficient package

For a company this size, a broker will usually bundle **GL + Media/Tech E&O** (add cyber) into one policy at a blended rate — cheaper and simpler than buying each separately. Start there.

## Steps

1. After the entity exists (Playbook 02), get 2–3 quotes from small-business insurers; describe the business honestly as "children's educational audio/media, online membership + school licensing."
2. Prioritize **Media Liability / E&O** in the conversation — it's the coverage most matched to your actual exposure.
3. Add **Cyber** before you hold significant member data.
4. Re-quote annually and whenever you add a major activity (live events, physical products, employees).

## Risk moves that aren't insurance

The cheapest risk reduction is upstream: the IP assignments in Playbook 08 (so you own what you publish), the licensed/original music (so no rights claims on the scores), the COPPA discipline in Playbook 05 (so a data incident is less likely and better handled), and the backups in Playbook 07. Insurance is the backstop, not the first line.
