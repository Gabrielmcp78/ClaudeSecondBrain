# Playbook 09 — Brand & Trademark

*Protecting the name, the mark, and the handles that carry the whole audience-trust strategy — before someone else uses "Shakespearience" or a look-alike, and before the brand is valuable enough to be worth taking.*

**Audience:** Stephanie + Sarah on brand; Gabriel + counsel on the filing.
**Last updated:** 2026-07-13
**Not legal advice** — a trademark attorney is worth it for the search and filing; the DIY path is possible but the clearance search is where amateurs miss conflicts.

---

## What you're protecting

- **The name** "The Shakespearience" (and the tagline "We are just human beings, having a Shakespearience").
- **The logo / wordmark** once Sarah finalizes it.
- **The domain and social handles** that must match the brand.

## Step 1 — Clearance search first

Before filing or committing spend, search that the name is actually available: the USPTO TESS database, a common-law web search, and domain/handle availability. A trademark attorney's clearance search is the high-value part — it catches confusingly-similar marks in your classes that a casual search misses. Do this before printing anything or announcing publicly.

## Step 2 — File the trademark (USPTO)

- Fee is **$350 per class** (2026 base). Pick classes deliberately — a media/education brand typically touches:
  - **Class 41** — education & entertainment services (the core: the podcast, educational content).
  - **Class 9** — downloadable media / software (the app, downloadable audio).
  - **Class 16** — printed matter (physical curriculum, guides) — add if/when you print.
- Start with **Class 41** (and likely 9); add 16 when the print products are real. Each class is a separate $350.
- If the mark isn't in commerce yet, file **intent-to-use**; a Statement of Use ($150/class) follows once you launch.
- Register the **©** side too: your recordings, scripts, and curriculum are copyrighted on creation, but registering key works strengthens enforcement.

## Step 3 — Defend the perimeter

- **Domain:** you own `shakespearience.com` (GoDaddy). Consider grabbing obvious variants (`.org`, common misspellings) defensively — cheap insurance.
- **Social handles:** claim the handle on every platform in the distribution plan (Apple, Spotify, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook) even before you post, so no one squats the name.
- **Watch:** once registered, a basic trademark-watch service flags infringers.

## Step 4 — Usage standards (Sarah's domain)

A short brand guide so the mark stays consistent everywhere: logo clear-space and minimum size, approved color values, typography, correct name usage ("The Shakespearience," capital S), and the tagline lockup. This protects the trademark (consistent use strengthens it) and the audience-trust brand equally.

## Timing

Clearance search → secure domain variants + handles (do immediately, they're cheap and get taken) → file Class 41 once the name is locked → add classes and Statement of Use as products ship. The handles and clearance search are the urgent, low-cost moves; the filing can follow the entity.
