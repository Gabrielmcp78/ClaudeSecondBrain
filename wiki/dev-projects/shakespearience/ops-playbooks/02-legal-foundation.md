# Playbook 02 — Legal Foundation

*Standing up the legal entity so the business can hold a bank account, sign with Stripe, own its work, and protect the founders personally. Everything financial depends on this being done first.*

**Audience:** Gabriel and Stephanie.
**Last updated:** 2026-07-13
**Not legal advice** — this is the practical checklist; confirm the specifics with a small-business attorney or a service like LegalZoom/Northwest before filing.

---

## The first decision: one entity or two?

Shakespearience is produced by **Present Company (Stephanie)**. Before anything else, settle how Shakespearience relates to it:

- **Option A — Shakespearience is a division of Present Company.** Simplest if Present Company is already an LLC and Stephanie owns the brand. Revenue and the founder split flow through Present Company's operating agreement. Gabriel's stake is defined by contract or membership interest inside it.
- **Option B — Shakespearience is its own LLC** co-owned by Gabriel and Stephanie. Cleaner separation of money, liability, and IP; the natural choice if this is a true 50/50-style partnership distinct from Present Company's other work.

Recommendation: **Option B** if the two of you are genuine co-owners of *this* venture — it keeps the cap table, banking, and IP unambiguous. Decide this with counsel; it drives every downstream step.

## What to file (Texas — Gabriel's base through 2026)

1. **Form the LLC** with the Texas Secretary of State (Certificate of Formation, Form 205). ~$300 state fee.
2. **Registered agent** — a person or service with a Texas address to receive legal mail. Use a service (~$100–125/yr) rather than a home address for privacy.
3. **EIN from the IRS** — free, done online in minutes once the LLC exists. This is the number the bank and Stripe require.
4. **Operating agreement** — the internal document defining ownership %, roles, decision rights, profit split, what happens if someone leaves, and how the brand/IP is owned. Do not skip this even for two friends; it is the thing that prevents the worst disputes.
5. **BOI / beneficial-ownership** reporting — confirm the current federal requirement with counsel at filing time, as the rules have shifted.

## Founder split — write it down

Capture in the operating agreement: ownership percentages, how Gabriel's technical build and Stephanie's creative/production work are valued, how the original music is licensed or assigned, decision authority (who signs, who has final creative vs. technical call), and a clean exit/buyout clause. The architecture doc already frames Gabriel as co-founder/CTO/composer and Stephanie as creator/producer — the operating agreement makes that legally real.

## IP ownership (critical, easy to miss)

Every contractor and actor agreement (see Playbook 08) must assign their work product to the company. Gabriel's original scores need explicit terms — either assigned to the entity or licensed to it — so the company clearly holds the rights it's monetizing. Absent this, the business is selling curriculum and audio it may not fully own.

## Order of operations

Entity formed → registered agent → EIN → operating agreement signed → **then** open the bank (Playbook 03) → then Stripe (Playbook 06). Nothing financial should be opened under a personal SSN or a not-yet-formed company.
