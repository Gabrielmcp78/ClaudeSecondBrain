# Playbook 08 — Contracts & People

*Making sure everyone who touches the production is paid clearly, taxed correctly, and — the part that quietly protects the whole business — has assigned their work to the company. A handshake is not a contract, and unassigned work is a rights problem waiting to surface.*

**Audience:** Gabriel and Stephanie (Stephanie leads casting; Gabriel leads the entity side).
**Last updated:** 2026-07-13
**Not legal advice** — use vetted templates or an attorney; the stakes here are ownership of the thing you're selling.

---

## The people to paper

- **Voice actors** (the 4–6 core ensemble) — performance + recording use.
- **Contractors** — Sarah (brand/graphic design), Jamie (social media), any editor or composer-for-hire beyond Gabriel.
- **Founders** — covered by the operating agreement (Playbook 02), including how Gabriel's original scores are owned/licensed.

## Every agreement must do three things

1. **Assign IP to the company.** A "work made for hire" clause plus a present assignment of rights so recordings, artwork, and copy belong to the entity — not the individual. Without this, a departing designer or actor could claim rights in what you're monetizing.
2. **Define the money.** Amount, schedule, and structure. Season 1 options already on the table: small stipends, deferred payment, or revenue share. Whichever you pick, write the exact terms and how/when revenue share is calculated and paid.
3. **Grant the usage rights you actually need** — distribution across all podcast platforms, curriculum, marketing, and future formats, in perpetuity, worldwide. Buying too narrow a license now forces renegotiation later.

## Actor agreements — specifics

- Performance scope (which season/episodes), session commitment, and re-record obligations.
- Usage: the audio can be distributed, excerpted for marketing, and included in paid curriculum.
- Compensation structure (stipend / deferred / revenue share) with clear math if it's a share.
- Credit and any moral-rights waivers appropriate to the format.
- Recording release so their recorded voice can be used as described.

## Contractor (1099) basics

- **Collect a W-9 before the first payment** from every US contractor; issue a **1099-NEC** in January for anyone paid $600+ in the year (bookkeeping in Playbook 03 tracks this).
- Classify correctly — independent contractors control their own means/method; misclassifying an employee as a contractor is a real liability. When unsure, ask the CPA.
- Each contractor agreement carries the same IP-assignment clause as above.

## Templates vs. attorney

Vetted templates (e.g. from a service, or a theatrical/podcast-specific source) cover the common case cheaply. Have an attorney review the **actor revenue-share** and **IP-assignment** language at least once — those two clauses are where a cheap mistake gets expensive. Store every signed agreement in the shared vault / Drive, never only in email.
