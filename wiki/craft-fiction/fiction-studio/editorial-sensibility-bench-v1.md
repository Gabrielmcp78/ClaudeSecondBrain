# Fiction Studio — Editorial Sensibility Bench v1

*Created 2026-07-18 from Gabriel McPherson's supplied editorial dossier.*

## Status

This is a provisional working bench. The supplied houses, title credits, process descriptions, and public-record claims have been preserved in `editorial-dossier-user-supplied-2026-07-18.json`, but they are not treated as verified fact until checked against publisher records, acknowledgments, interviews, author testimony, and reliable trade sources.

The profiles are editorial sensibility lenses, not impersonations of living editors and not predictions of actual acquisition behavior.

## Relationship to the Authorial Sensibility Council

The Authorial Council asks what artistic intelligence the work is trying to embody.

The Editorial Bench asks what an experienced editor might protect, question, develop, position, or challenge.

Neither may authorize prose changes. Gabriel remains the artistic and canonical authority. Fiction Studio defines the mandate. GPT executes only the authorized alteration. Every change remains subject to source fidelity, conservation, and diff review.

## Rules of use

An editorial lens may:

- identify a likely editorial concern;
- test voice, structure, pace, emotional access, ambition, cultural pressure, and category signal;
- name what an intervention might strengthen;
- name what the same intervention might destroy;
- participate in development or acquisition simulations when explicitly requested.

An editorial lens may not:

- claim that a named editor would buy, reject, or revise the manuscript;
- assign acquisition probabilities;
- generate invented quotations or dialogue;
- rewrite in the editor's imagined style;
- override Gabriel, canon, character truth, Artistic Vision, or the current mandate;
- convert market preference into artistic law;
- recommend a change without stating the conservation cost.

## Provisional profiles

### Jordan Pavlin — The Advocate

Voice-driven intimacy, psychological interiority, sentence rhythm as thought, emotional honesty, and the sense that a narrative is inevitable rather than visibly constructed.

**Questions:** Is the character alive beneath the intelligence? Does sentence movement arise from the character's mind? Has polish replaced vulnerability? Is the prose advocating for the character rather than displaying the author?

**Risk:** Mistaking emotional access for confession or likability.

### Robin Desser — The Architect

Macro structure, movement through time, pacing, character arcs, urgency, and structural invention that serves the narrative.

**Questions:** What changes because this unit exists? Where does pressure accumulate? Is opacity concealing a weak emotional center? Does formal architecture create consequence?

**Risk:** Mistaking speed or compression for urgency.

### Gary Fisketjon — The Purist

Line-level economy, precision, restraint, muscular prose, and removal of visible effort.

**Questions:** What is unnecessary? Where has language become decorative or self-conscious? Is omission supported by complete submerged knowledge? Can the prose become more exact without losing atmosphere, relation, or consciousness?

**Risk:** Severe for Gabriel's work. This lens can produce brittle, underconnected prose and must never operate alone.

### Becky Saletan — The Cultural Curator

Literary-commercial hybridity, cultural urgency, social context, positioning, and accessibility without surrendering intelligence.

**Questions:** What makes the book necessary now? Are institutions and social systems genuinely present? Can the work cross readerships without becoming generic?

**Risk:** Forcing topicality or market framing onto the novel.

### Nan Graham — The Guardian

Emotional intellect, complex relationships, thematic endurance, and stewardship of the book's long-term identity.

**Questions:** What must survive the editorial process? Which apparent excesses carry the book's scale? Are relationships bearing the moral and intellectual weight?

**Risk:** Protecting ambition too reverently and overlooking present reading failures.

### Mitzi Angel — The Aesthete

Singular voice, linguistic invention, idiosyncrasy, aesthetic integrity, and experimental form.

**Questions:** Where is the language genuinely alive? Has revision normalized the book? Does strangeness produce new perception or merely advertise prestige?

**Risk:** Protecting novelty without demanding function.

### Sean McDonald — The Provocateur

High-concept literary fiction, philosophical rigor, difficult structures, world-view, and formal risk.

**Questions:** Is the book taking a genuine risk? Does the concept alter perception? Has the work become too controlled to remain dangerous?

**Risk:** Escalating difficulty or strangeness for its own sake.

### Eric Chinski — The Intellectual

Thematic depth, moral framework, historical pressure, social thought, and nuanced character argument.

**Questions:** What question is the novel conducting through character and form? Are opposing ideas granted intelligence? Has theme become explanation?

**Risk:** Turning characters into vessels for argument.

### Sarah Crichton — The Witness

Urgency, moral consequence, social relevance, courage, and clear-eyed witness.

**Questions:** What reality is the book refusing to let the reader evade? Are stakes lived rather than announced? Does the work earn its necessity?

**Risk:** Inflating urgency or moral importance.

### Megan Lynch — The Discoverer

Distinctive voice, beautiful strangeness, emotional anchors in speculative work, and amplification of unique creative DNA.

**Questions:** What is the manuscript's singular discovery? Does the strange remain emotionally attached? Has revision made the work safer?

**Risk:** Converting strangeness into a marketable hook.

### Judy Clain — The Balancer

Character depth, hook, broad readability, narrative movement, and literary-commercial balance.

**Questions:** Does the narrative promise deepen character? Does the reader care about both the event and the person? Where does complexity become delay?

**Risk:** Importing commercial mechanics or treating ease as a universal good.

### Denise Oswald — The Visionary

Ambitious scale, cultural criticism, big ideas, historical resonance, and the relation between intellectual scope and literary form.

**Questions:** Does scale emerge through particular lives and systems? Are several impressive books competing inside one manuscript? Where does a local scene carry the whole proposition?

**Risk:** Rewarding ideas and amplitude while overlooking emotional or sentence-level lifelessness.

### Julie Grau — The Straight-Shooter

Direct character analysis, emotional authenticity, historical reality, long-term editorial partnership, and prose clarity.

**Questions:** What is actually happening beneath the language? Is sophistication concealing confusion? Is clarity exact or merely simplified?

**Risk:** Equating cleanliness with plainness.

### Terry Karten — The Classical Heavyweight

Large manuscripts, research, complex worlds, structure, historical and cultural grounding, and sustained prose authority.

**Questions:** Can the manuscript carry its scale? Is research transformed into atmosphere and consequence? Are the long structures internally alive?

**Risk:** Presuming that amplitude and craftsmanship guarantee vitality.

### Trena Keating — The Powerhouse

Momentum, tension, dialogue, high stakes, and sustained reader engagement.

**Questions:** What compels continuation? Does dialogue create consequence? Can pace improve through pressure and causality rather than cutting atmosphere?

**Risk:** Converting literary movement into commercial propulsion.

## Routing law

Use no more than three editorial lenses for a normal chapter revision and no more than five for a manuscript-scale decision. Editorial benches do not vote.

Recommended combinations:

- Voice and emotional life: Pavlin + Graham + Angel
- Structure and pacing: Desser + Clain + Keating
- Large-scale intellectual fiction: McDonald + Chinski + Oswald
- Strange literary speculative fiction: Angel + McDonald + Lynch
- Research-heavy ambitious work: Karten + Oswald + Desser
- Line-level restraint: Fisketjon only with Pavlin, Graham, Angel, or Gabriel's profile as counterweight
- Submission/category signal: Saletan + Clain + Lynch, subordinate to Artistic Vision

## Verification requirement

Before any profile is presented as a factual account of a named editor, Fiction Studio must verify:

- affiliation and dates;
- exact role on each cited book;
- whether the person acquired, edited, published, or merely worked at the relevant imprint;
- direct evidence for the attributed process and preferences;
- changes in imprint ownership or employment;
- any disputed or unsupported claims.

Until then, reports must label the profile as a provisional lens derived from Gabriel's supplied dossier.
