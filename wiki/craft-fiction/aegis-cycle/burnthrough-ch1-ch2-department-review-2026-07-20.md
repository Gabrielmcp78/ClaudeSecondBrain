## Executive Judgment

The Prologue plus Chapters 1 and 2 form a stronger opening engine than Chapters 1-2 alone. The Prologue gives the reader immediate velocity, cargo mystery, leaked-route pressure, offstage antagonist reach, the administrator's compromised silence, cryonic countdown, and the first hard Dock 9 alarm. Chapter 1 can then widen into Erevos, Kieran's return, Draven's authority, ARIA, and the Collective crate without bearing the entire burden of first threat by itself.

The pages announce the correct book: adult epic space opera driven by diaspora infrastructure, technical systems, queer power and unresolved intimacy, military hierarchy, smuggling competence, and institutional secrets. The opening does not need a new premise.

The highest-leverage issue remains hierarchy, not invention. The Prologue is cleanly functional as a three-part pressure setup, but it has a duplicate `2. Bounty` heading, its administrator name `Silas Renn` must be replaced, and it depends on later source discipline around Veryn / Veyr and Dock 9. Chapter 1 carries world history, station identity, Aegis personality, Kieran competence, Draven heat, Asha authority, ARIA intimacy, Dock 9 foreshadowing, Collective evidence, and the first investigation turn. Most of that material is doing real work, but it currently competes for foreground. Chapter 2 successfully converts the opening into an active investigation, though it also reveals continuity and terminology debt that must be cleaned before a serious line edit.

Official decision: protect the opening architecture and perform a controlled hierarchy/source-control pass. Gabriel confirmed on 2026-07-20 that the 3.0 split Chapter 1-2 files are the live early-chapter source.

## Department Findings

### Source Control and Canon Authority

Finding: The Prologue is canon-safe evidence against consolidated 2.3; Chapters 1-2 remain usable for diagnosis but not fully canon-locked. The 3.0 split files should not be silently merged with consolidated 2.3.

Evidence: `Prologue.docx` is a 100% paragraph match with the consolidated 2.3 prologue. Consolidated 2.3 also contains the early `Erevos Rising` / `Paper Scissors` range, but the 3.0 Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 files are cleaned and expanded. The 3.0 Chapter 1 includes an early Dock 9 glance during the hangar walk, which is structurally important for the later Jalen/Soren map. The current-state board still requires Gabriel confirmation of consolidated 2.3 vs. 3.0 alternate vs. hybrid authority.

Severity: meaningful issue.

Recommendation: required before revision. Treat the Prologue as verified opening source. Confirm whether the 3.0 Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 files are the live working versions for early chapters, or whether they are insertion candidates for the consolidated shell.

### Narrative Architecture

Finding: The opening has strong architecture. The Prologue correctly gives the book threat, speed, and cargo consequence before Chapter 1 shifts into return, station identity, and intimacy under authority. The remaining architecture risk is that Chapter 1 still foregrounds too many systems at once.

Evidence: The Prologue moves from Drosshaven escape to unmanifested cryonic cargo, leaked route, failed interception, administrator coercion, and restricted bay C-17 delivery under Draven's authorization. It ends on the Dock 9 temperature warning. Chapter 1 moves from Erevos history to Aegis arrival, cargo deception, Draven interception, Dock 9 unease, Asha's surveillance reveal, ARIA access, mesh receivers, and the Collective crate. Chapter 2 then advances into ARIA integration, station-grid anomaly, power-distribution warning, and Bazaar fieldwork.

Severity: meaningful issue, not blocking.

Recommendation: optional only after source lock. A hierarchy pass should clarify which revelations are first-order and which are secondary texture. Do not remove the Prologue's cryonic clock, administrator coercion, Dock 9 alarm, ARIA, Draven/Kieran, Asha, or the crate.

### Character Psychology

Finding: The Prologue effectively introduces Kieran and Tovan as high-functioning chaos before naming them, then Chapters 1-2 deepen Kieran's defensive charm, erotic volatility, and technical brilliance. The risk is that some later narrator-asides explain his defenses after the scene has already dramatized them.

Evidence: The escape banter exposes Kieran/Tovan's trust and panic-management without explanation. The dialogue with Draven and Asha makes Kieran's strategy visible again under command pressure. By contrast, some commentary around charm, annoyance, and Asha's reaction names the social effect more directly than the page needs.

Severity: observation to meaningful issue.

Recommendation: in any later line pass, preserve the banter and failed defenses, but reduce explanatory commentary where dialogue, timing, body heat, or operational pressure already carries the truth.

### Reader Experience

Finding: The Prologue substantially improves reader propulsion. It gives a clean reason to keep reading before the station exposition begins. The main reader risk remains front-loaded proper-noun and system density across the full opening package.

Evidence: By the end of Chapter 2, the reader has encountered Drosshaven, Erevos, Vaylos Span, Dock 9, restricted bay C-17, Sehrava III/IV, New Aureth Chain, ARIA, NOHCM, Celerian/Celeriac Collective, Carth Belt, Fen Olik, Commander Veryn/Veyr, cryonic matrix containers, isotope injection viability, power lattice anomalies, receivers, ghost manifests, and the Bazaar vector.

Severity: meaningful issue.

Recommendation: orientation economy pass. Keep the difficulty and scale, but decide which terms must be learned now and which can arrive through consequence later.

### Prestige Prose

Finding: The prose has a distinctive engine: swagger, sensuality, machinery, institutional dread, and comic vulgarity are integrated rather than decorative. The Prologue proves this voice can move fast without becoming generic. The risk remains image clustering in Chapter 1, not in the Prologue.

Evidence: The Drosshaven escape dialogue carries speed and character at once. Renn's coercion scene shifts into institutional menace with a different pressure profile. The station opening, Draven's entrance, the Dock 9 image, ARIA's "beautiful brain" exchange, and the crate reveal all carry voice and pressure. But several Chapter 1 paragraphs stack metaphor, technical naming, irony, and sexual voltage at once, which can make the strongest images compete with nearby cleverness.

Severity: meaningful issue.

Recommendation: if revision is authorized later, perform a hierarchy pass rather than a simplification pass. Clear space around the strongest images; do not flatten syntax into short neutral action beats.

### Continuity and Canon

Finding: The Prologue confirms several terminology and name conflicts are opening-level issues, not later cleanup. Gabriel has now locked Kieran Vale and Tovan Pyrahn; the administrator currently named Silas Renn must be renamed.

Evidence: The Prologue uses `Commander Veryn` for the restricted bay delivery. Chapters 1-2 and support records introduce `Veyr/Veryn` pressure. The antagonist appears as both Celeriac and Celerian Collective across the live BURNThrough package. The forged signature appears as `KIELAN VALE`, which may be typo or intentional false identity. The surveillance example differs between source versions as `Tolan Brek` / `Tolan Vrek`; Gabriel clarified this should remain separate from Tovan Pyrahn unless `Tolan` is retained as a minor incidental character. The Prologue also contains a duplicate `2. Bounty` heading, and `Silas Renn` is flagged for replacement because the name echoes Star Wars too closely.

Severity: meaningful issue.

Recommendation: required continuity cleanup. Apply [BURNThrough Opening Name Ledger](burnthrough-opening-name-ledger-2026-07-20.md) before revising these chapters.

### Scientific and Domain Review

Finding: The technical system is promising because it is dramatic rather than decorative: cryonic cargo, isotope-injection viability, ARIA, NOHCM, ghost manifests, receivers, power anomalies, and station-grid behavior all generate action. The issue is terminology discipline.

Evidence: The Prologue's kelvin readings and five-day injection countdown establish concrete procedural pressure. Chapter 2's 587-stream overload, station-grid reading, receiver pattern, and power-distribution warning all connect Kieran's competence to the plot. Some phrases lean toward stacked pseudo-technical density and should be governed by a bible rather than line-by-line improvisation.

Severity: observation to meaningful issue.

Recommendation: create or update a systems mini-bible for ARIA, NOHCM, mesh receivers, biophotonic / phot-neuronic terminology, and authentication rules.

### Editorial Board

Finding: With the Prologue included, the opening's professional signal is stronger. The book makes an immediate proposition before the reader is asked to learn the station. It is still not ready for broad professional reading until source authority, continuity terms, and opening hierarchy are tightened.

Evidence: The pages make the central proposition legible: dangerous cargo escapes Drosshaven, powerful actors erase and buy consequences, the cargo arrives under Draven's authority, Kieran returns to a compromised home, an old lover now has command authority over him, a station-scale system is already under attack, and his intimacy with ARIA is both asset and vulnerability. The professional risk is not lack of ambition; it is avoidable friction from source/copy inconsistency, over-frontloaded terminology, and a prologue administrator name that creates an unwanted franchise echo.

Severity: meaningful issue.

Recommendation: diagnose and source-clean before line revision; do not reposition the book into a simpler action thriller.

### Artistic Vision

Preservation Challenge: Do not "fix" the opening by civilizing out its swagger, heat, vulgarity, or excessive-sounding life. The Prologue demonstrates that the voice can survive high-speed action; Chapters 1-2 demonstrate that the same life can carry desire, shame, systems, and command pressure. Those are not surface flavor; they are the mechanism by which Kieran survives authority, shame, desire, and danger.

Cost of preserving it: the opening will remain more intense and more linguistically crowded than safer commercial space opera.

Cost of normalizing it: Kieran becomes a generic rogue captain, Draven becomes generic command tension, and ARIA becomes a tool instead of an intimate system.

Finding: The review remained diagnostic and did not generate substitute prose. The next pass must not begin until source authority is confirmed.
