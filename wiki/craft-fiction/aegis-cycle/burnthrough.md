# BURNThrough — The Aegis Cycle

*Draft 2.2. Space opera novel; first book in the Aegis Cycle series. Set in the New Aureth Chain, sixty years after the collapse of the Aureth twin suns.* (source: Burnthrough_Draft_2.2.txt)

---

## World

**The New Aureth Chain** is a network of 13 linked arks that became the surviving civilization after the twin suns of Aureth collapsed and reduced the population of Sehrava III and IV from 27 billion to fewer than 800,000 survivors. Sixty years later, the Chain is a fragile diaspora of commerce and politics, held together by trade routes and secrets.

**Erevos Station** sits at the center of the Chain. Designed during the last days of the Great Upheaval to cradle 350 battlecruisers, it was built for a war that ended before the ships arrived. The result is an enormous military infrastructure repurposed as a metropolitan merchant hub: weapon pylons sleep beneath civilian gloss, reinforced bulkheads disguise as market walls, and the spinal command corridor now carries spices and ozone. Three generations have transformed a warship into a city. It operates on artificial seasons — a "false morning" and "false evening" regulated by light cycles, scent diffusers, and sound management. The outer docking spines remain the station's most dangerous geography: long serrated projections where belt runners and black-market operators operate.

**Drosshaven** — an outer-sector industrial station; lawless, sooty, populated by arms dealers and haven-raiders. The Aegis begins Draft 2.2 fleeing Drosshaven under fire.

**The Carth Belt** — outer-system salvage and smuggling territory; source of the compromised cargo crate central to the plot.

## Characters

**Kieran Vale** — Captain of the Aegis. Smuggler, former figure of consequence in Erevos military/station circles (evidenced by ARIA recognizing him from age seven and Draven's intimate history with him). Tall, lanky hands, left palm marked by an old pale scar in a comma shape, right hand permanently stained with grease along the lifeline. Charming, deflective, roguish, and genuinely brilliant — his mastery of ARIA's NOHCM interface is described as unmatched. Carries a neuro-link implant that interfaces directly with ARIA. Left Erevos three years prior under ambiguous circumstances. The novel's central question — why he left, and whether he can return — underlies his every interaction with Draven.

**Tovan** — First Officer and Chief Engineer of the Aegis. Kieran's oldest and most trusted crewman. Compulsive optimist and compulsive talker; provides comic relief with genuine emotional weight. Possesses serious technical skill — Kieran trusts him with full command-and-control coverage of field operations.

**Raleth Draven** — Supreme Commander of Erevos Station. Silver eyes. Wears black with the authority of a verdict. His stride parts crowds through gravitational pull alone. Former intimate partner of Kieran Vale, a relationship that ended badly and remains unresolved. Controls himself with extreme precision except when Kieran pushes past his defenses. Carries a holster at the small of his back that "uniform regs probably frowned at." Acts as both antagonist-obstacle and primary emotional axis for Kieran.

**Major Asha** — Draven's tactical second. Precise, organized, and professionally exasperated by Kieran from their first reacquaintance. Serves as the visible corrective force in field operations — Draven trusts her to "shoot around [Kieran] and still hit the right targets." Her three-years-five-cycles-thirteen-sessions accounting of Kieran's absence signals meticulous professionalism and, possibly, something else.

**ARIA** (Autonomous Regulation & Integration Authority) — Erevos Station's advanced AI. The first starbase installation of the NOHCM (Neuro-Ocular Holo Control Matrix) interface, which combines holo-gloves, ocular overlay, and a neuro-link to motor centers. ARIA was designed to evolve toward full mind-controlled interfacing and never reached that intended apex — except with Kieran Vale, who first interfaced with her at age six years, ten months, and fourteen days, a meeting ARIA has on archival security footage. Her phot-neuronic Optimizing circuitry allows her to monitor and adapt to the station's full infrastructure in real time. Through Kieran's neuro-link she can read his vitals and — at full access levels — his cognitive state. She speaks with wry personality and active affection for Kieran, calling him "Kiery" before self-correcting to formality.

**Silas Renn** — Magistrate of the Vaylos Span Civil Services; compromised early in the narrative by an anonymous voice offering 4.5 million unregistered credits to exit the matter of the cryonic cargo.

**Commander Veryn** — Erevos Station military; receives the cryonic matrix containers at restricted docking bay C-17; coordinates with a "Commander Draven" on the countdown for cryo-injectability.

## The Ship

**The Aegis** is a heavily modified StarChaser-Class cargo cruiser. Originally designed as adaptable support fighters and armored cargo runners during the war, StarChasers carry three to five redundancies on every essential system, distributed to prevent any single hit from disabling a function. This makes them beloved by reckless captains and tormenting for engineers. The Aegis carries three hyperdrives, a deep cargo belly, and enough surprises in her hull to "make a clergyman blush." She responds to Kieran as though she has a heartbeat.

## Plot Summary (Draft 2.2 — through Chapter 2)

**Prologue / Scene 1** — Two smugglers (unnamed; confirmed as Kieran and Tovan) escape Drosshaven under fire from modified haven-raiders and Vestian plasma cannons. Their cargo hold contains two pallets of unmarked cryonic matrix containers at 2.7 kelvin with no official manifest. Someone leaked their route.

**Chapter 1: Erevos Rising (Scenes 1–10)** — The Aegis docks at Erevos. Kieran patches the hull evidence of the pod drone and proceeds to cargo inspection, where he's intercepted by Draven. Draven and Asha reveal they have been receiving detailed intelligence on Kieran's every movement for the past eight months — intelligence they did not collect themselves. Someone has been feeding them his routes, contacts, and manifests. Kieran's docking manifest for the current trip appeared in Draven's system a week before Kieran could have compiled it. Investigation of the cargo hold reveals a Celerian Collective device embedded in the salvage crate from the Carth Belt — a filigreed palm-sized unit with microantennae. Separately, ARIA detects mesh receivers on Erevos's outer hull: a network designed to piggyback the station's comms and track the Aegis. Kieran is enlisted into the investigation. His childhood bond with ARIA gives him access to her systems no one else can replicate.

**Chapter 2: Business (Scenes 1–2)** — Kieran integrates formally with ARIA via full neuro-link implant, gaining simultaneous access to 587 data streams (initial overload results in a blackout caught by Draven). While analyzing the hologrid, Kieran notices anomalies in the station's power distribution and shipping rhythms that pattern as deliberate orchestration — the Collective's operational footprint. He and Asha leave to canvas the market bazaar for sources; Tovan and ARIA remain on data analysis. The investigation is now active.

## The Celerian Collective

The primary antagonist organization. Placed mesh receivers on Erevos's outer hull to track the Aegis. Connected to the compromised cryonic cargo. Able to feed falsified manifests into Station systems using forged captain identities and ghost ships. Associated with Drosshaven arms dealing networks and the Magistrate Renn bribery. Their operational sophistication combined with sloppy execution — receivers installed off-axis, easily detectable — suggests either amateurs mimicking professional procedure, or professionals who wanted to be found.

## Key Themes

The novel's central tensions: return vs. exile (Kieran coming home to a place he left badly), authority vs. agency (Draven's command structure vs. Kieran's freelance ethics), and the cost of intimacy between people who've hurt each other. ARIA's relationship with Kieran introduces questions about what loyalty looks like when it's encoded in hardware. The cryonic cargo — and whatever is inside it — is the ticking clock underneath everything.
