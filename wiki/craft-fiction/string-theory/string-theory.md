## Core Premise

David Lang spent twenty years making sure the world would never have to hear what he discovered. He almost succeeded.

As a physicist, David buried a research discovery alongside his collaborators (Celeste Armitage, Marcus McGrath) in a literal time capsule beneath a Cornell bench, under institutional pressure from Rune Ishikawa. He built a prestigious academic career on top of that silence. As the novel opens, his father Arthur has just died — the one relationship David never managed to understand — and the buried phenomenon begins surfacing again.

The phenomenon is physical (a 68.48 Hz frequency), familial (David's grandfather Elliot carried the same sensitivity), and historical (Project ARGO, 1971, uncovered it first). By the novel's climax on David's 47th birthday, it is no longer possible to contain.

## Central Characters

- **David Lang** — physicist, Feynman Chair at Cornell, former cellist. The convergence point.
- **Celeste Armitage** — violist, co-researcher, ex-partner. The relationship David cannot escape.
- **Arthur Lang** — David's father, deceased. Former BSO cellist. The silence David inherited.
- **Rune Ishikawa** — department chair; appears antagonistic through Chapter 8; recontextualized in Chapter 17 as protector.
- **Eleanor Guare** — instrument repair shop proprietor (Buffalo); revealed in Chapter 5 to be a 1971 Project ARGO researcher.
- **Marcus McGrath** — third co-researcher; panic-destructor of their 2005 papers; named President of the SolPhi Institute in the aftermath.
