# The 68.48 Hz Mechanic

The 68.48 Hz frequency is the central physical phenomenon of *String Theory*. It is the resonant frequency of the time capsule buried beneath the bench on Cornell's campus quad, and the mechanism through which the novel's science fiction premise operates. (source: string-theory-chapter-01.md)

---

## Physical Description

68.48 Hz sits between C#2 (69.30 Hz) and C2 (65.41 Hz) — "almost half the fine-structure," as David Lang describes it. It is not a standard tuning pitch. It is experienced by David as:

- A low-frequency buzz that arrives unbidden in his consciousness
- A sensation that scratches at the skull, vibrates the molars, pulses through the sternum
- Directional — it intensifies and localizes as David approaches the burial site
- Overwhelming at the bench, where it causes David to emit a primal scream that shakes windows and stone

The sensation is known rather than heard; David identifies it as 68.48 Hz without measurement.

---

## The Buried Capsule

The frequency originates from a time capsule buried six feet beneath the bench on Cornell's campus quad by David Lang, Celeste Armitage, and Marcus McGrath during their graduate school years (approximately 20 years before the novel's present). The capsule contains their acoustic physics manifestos. The capsule was buried with an agreement: "if it's real, it'll claw its way out."

A Polaroid taken at the burial — a tuning fork in soil — later appears inexplicably in David's lecture slides, the first anomalous intrusion of the capsule into David's present.

---

## Multigenerational Transmission

Chapter 2 reveals that David's grandfather Elliot Lang possessed "the same mysterious melody David has unconsciously known his entire life." This detail suggests the 68.48 Hz phenomenon is not acquired from the capsule but is an inherited sensitivity — the capsule may be an anchor or amplifier rather than a source. [theoretical]

## Temporal Effects

The frequency correlates with temporal distortions throughout Chapters 1–2:

- David's watch loses five minutes on the day the frequency first appears
- Clock readings throughout Chapter 1 do not align with experienced duration
- By Chapter 2 David cannot determine whether his watch reads seconds, hours, or "one minute, forty-six seconds before time"

Whether the temporal distortion is a symptom of the frequency itself or of David's psychological response to it is unresolved. [unverified]
