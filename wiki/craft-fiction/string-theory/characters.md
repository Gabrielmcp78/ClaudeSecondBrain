# String Theory — Characters

Primary and secondary characters across *String Theory* by Gabriel McPherson. Chapter references reflect the current 27-chapter Draft 6.7 build unless marked [6.6: Ch X]. See [Novel Structure](novel-structure.md) for the full chapter mapping.

---

## David Lang

Physicist; holder of the Feynman Chair of Quantum Dynamics at Cornell University. Former cellist of near-professional caliber. Ex-partner of Celeste Armitage (20+ years, never married). Son of Arthur Lang, grandson of Elliot Lang.

David is the novel's central figure — a man suspended between two vocations, neither of which he can fully inhabit. His physics output has stalled after early brilliance; his cello playing is technically flawless but emotionally absent. He experiences the [68.48 Hz frequency](68hz-mechanic.md) beginning Chapter 1, which correlates with proximity to the buried capsule beneath the campus quad bench at Cornell.

His father Arthur died two weeks before Chapter 1 opens. His grandfather Elliot Lang possessed the same mysterious melody David has known unconsciously his entire life (revealed Chapter 2), suggesting the phenomenon is inherited, not acquired.

David's watch runs behind; clock readings disagree with experienced time. By Chapter 2 his equations drift into musical notation without his conscious control and his temporal perception collapses at Cornell's corridor level.

The equation he writes on his apartment chalkboard and preserves: **RESONANCE = SURVIVAL**.

---

## Celeste Armitage

Violist; the quartet's anchor. David's ex-partner. Co-author (with David and Marcus McGrath) of the 2005 theoretical work on acoustic resonance and consciousness that triggered institutional attention.

Celeste is emotionally direct, observant, and grounded — the counterweight to David's abstraction. In Chapter 1's rehearsal she pleads: "David… stay with us. Stay with me." The words carry the full weight of years of watching him evaporate into equations. In the 2005 MIT flashback she is the preservationist of the group's research, mediating between David's drive and Marcus's destructive panic.

## Eleanor Guare (E. Guare)

First encountered in Chapter 6 ("Alteration Finds") as the proprietor of an instrument repair shop in Buffalo — the shop appears across Ch 6 scenes 6.1 (The Instrument Shop) and 6.2 (The Workshop). Displays cryptic familiarity with David. Her name appears in Chapter 20's title ("Phenom de Guare"), marking that chapter as her focal point. Chapter 12 ("Twin Primes on the six") reveals her as a 1971 Project ARGO researcher — "researchers Tsukino and Guare."

The repair marks inside David's cello case (dated 1985, bearing his grandfather Elliot Lang's initials) suggest a connection to the Lang family predating David's awareness of her. The nature and depth of that connection is a primary unresolved thread.

## Dr. Rune Ishikawa

Department chair; "guardian of East Hill" at Cornell. First violinist — no; department chair of physics. A figure of institutional authority and strategic ambiguity.

In Chapter 1 he interrupts David's lecture with pointed questions about harmony versus resonance, implies David's current work lacks the speculative rigor of his early output, and exits with the provocation: "Resonance equals survival?" In the 2005 flashback he arrives at the MIT bonfire to contain the research, demanding David, Marcus, and Celeste bring all materials to his Cornell office. His language operates with surgical precision; he gives nothing while extracting everything.

Whether Ishikawa is antagonist, handler, or something more ambiguous is unresolved as of Chapter 2.

---

## Arthur Lang (deceased)

David's father. Former second chair with the Boston Symphony Orchestra. Wanted music for David, not physics — the road David did not fully take. Died approximately two weeks before Chapter 1. His unexecuted will sits in David's office in a manila envelope David cannot bring himself to open. When David calls his father's phone, the answering machine greeting plays; the impossibility of the moment is the chapter's emotional nadir.

David sees his father's face overlaying his own in the mirror — "an inheritance of features he can no longer deny."

---

## Elliot Lang (deceased)

David's grandfather. Possessed a mysterious melody that David has known unconsciously his entire life — revealed by David's mother Cassandra in Chapters 5–8 (the "Edge of Discovery" material distributed across the Now…Then/Alteration Finds/Firebirds/Remaining Returns sequence of the current build). His initials appear in repair marks inside David's cello case dated 1985, connecting him to Eleanor Guare's instrument shop. His knowledge of acoustic phenomena was secretive and specialized. The cross-generational transmission of the melody suggests the 68.48 Hz phenomenon is inherited. *(Draft 6.6: Cassandra's revelation was in Ch 2.)*

---

## Marcus McGrath

Third member of the 2005 research group alongside David and Celeste. In the MIT flashback he attempts to suppress their theoretical work through physical destruction — burning papers at a bonfire. The impulse to destroy suggests fear rather than disagreement. His current whereabouts and relationship to David as of the novel's present are not established in Chapters 1–2.

He was also present at the original capsule burial beneath the Cornell bench, making him one of three signatories to the agreement: "if it's real, it'll claw its way out."

---

## Dr. Julian Kline

NYU astrophysicist. His reaction to David's 2005 presentation triggered institutional concern serious enough to bring Rune Ishikawa to the MIT bonfire. He appears in the present-day Buffalo conference sequence with visible dread upon seeing David. The nature of what he recognized in David's 2005 research is unresolved.

---

## Cassandra Lang

David's mother, still living. Remembers her father-in-law Elliot Lang's secretive behavior around the melody. Her Chapter 2 revelation — that Elliot possessed the same melody David has always known — is the first confirmation that the phenomenon is multigenerational.

---

## Roger Linn

First violinist; Professor of String Performance at Cornell. David's friend for 30+ years. Tactful and protective of the quartet's equilibrium. In Chapter 1 he and Eleanor Rieger excuse themselves after David's C-string snaps, giving David and Celeste space.

---

## Eleanor Rieger

Second violinist; diplomat-like warmth. Offers condolences about Arthur Lang's death at the Chapter 1 rehearsal.

---

## Dr. Kenji Tsukino

Lead of Project ARGO at Cornell, 1971. Disappeared after Run 47's anomalous output. Never directly encountered in the novel's present; known only through the archived Overture document.

---

## Ralph

Custodian at Feynman Hall. Philosopher-janitor who cleans the building at night. Offers David wisdom about resonance and survival.

---

## Professor Garrin

Cornell colleague; minor character, first appearing (unnamed classroom owner) roughly nine months before Chapter 13's present action and directly on-page in Chapter 13 ("The Purge"). David steals a chair from Garrin's classroom mid-lecture — the second in a pair, having taken its match nine months earlier in what David rationalizes as "gentleman's larceny." Garrin is left mid-diagram at the board, unable to reach his own wall phone without crossing the room, and eventually reports the incident to the Dean's office. Comic-institutional counterweight to David's escalating unraveling; no larger role confirmed yet. (source: chapter-13-full-text-2026-07-16)
