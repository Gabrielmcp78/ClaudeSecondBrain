# Voice Doctrine — Celeste & Cassandra (author-established 2026-07-20)

**Source:** Gabriel, verbal, during the Ch.9 hinge redesign session (Claude/Fiction Studio). Canon-level character doctrine. Governs all drafting and revision of both characters, retroactive and forward.

## The doctrine

Celeste and Cassandra are the same person expressed through different instruments.

- **Cassandra is a singer: everything comes out of her mouth.** Speech-dominant. Overflow, projection to the rear of the hall, self-interruption, tangents, no detectable filter. She conducts rooms (and sons) verbally and indirectly — staging, implying, never quite issuing the instruction. On the page she exists largely as pure voice (the phone), and the form should keep matching the doctrine.
- **Celeste is a player: everything comes out of her body.** Body-dominant. Touch, posture, positioning, hands, bow, the practiced flourish, the interior pitch, silence. Her sentences trail, break, or get replaced by an action; when she completes one, it carries the weight of a downbeat. She is the same intelligence as Cassandra, educated differently and never garish about it.
- They recognize each other instantly. Cassandra's "If anybody would understand it, she would" is self-recognition in another temperament. When Cassandra stages something indirectly (the book in the parlor), **Celeste identifies the maneuver at once — with a look, never a line — because it is her own move-set transposed to voice.**

## Drafting rules derived

1. Celeste must not win exchanges through spoken wit. Verbal fencing belongs to David (and the academic sport of Rune/Sweeney). Celeste parries briefly; her true answers are physical or musical.
2. Cassandra's comedy comes from unfiltered overflow and from not recognizing David's rules as binding — never from clipped aphorism.
3. In any scene containing both women (even one by phone), the channels must contrast on the page: her voice fills the room; her body changes it.
4. David's channel, for the record: everything comes out of him as equations — the wrong instrument for what he is carrying. The book's tragedy in one clause.
5. The novel's thesis restated as character design: same signal, different instruments. Mouth and body. Song and string.
