# Project ARGO

Project ARGO (Autonomous Resonance Generation and Observation) was a classified research project conducted at Cornell University in Fall 1971 by six researchers across five disciplines. It is introduced in the novel's Overture as a "fragment recovered from sealed archive." (source: string-theory-overture.md)

---

## Premise

The researchers investigated whether "structure can recognize itself across impossible distances." Inputs were selected for symmetry: star positions, ritual calendars, obsolete tuning systems, linguistic trees. Over 46 experimental runs, the system produced only noise.

---

## Run 47

Run 47 initiated autonomously at 01:47 a.m. — not triggered by the researchers. Its output:

> THE FIRST MARK OF THE SECOND ORDER IS NOT WHERE YOU LEFT IT

The output was assembled from incompatible datasets. The researchers concluded the system was not generating output but "mirroring" it, and that the phenomenon represented an "aperture" rather than a transmission.

---

## Aftermath

Dr. Kenji Tsukino, project lead, disappeared. The project was sealed. The remaining staff signed undisclosed relocation agreements. The terminal continued displaying an evolving 32-line glyph every 47 seconds.

The final recovered note stated the researchers had overlooked that resonance requires reciprocity: they had "tuned to something" which subsequently "looked back."

---

## Significance

Project ARGO establishes the novel's operating premise: the phenomenon is not being discovered but recognized; the researchers are not initiating contact but responding to something that is already looking back. The 1971 date predates David Lang's birth, connecting the phenomenon to an institutional history at Cornell that David is only beginning to encounter as a faculty member.
