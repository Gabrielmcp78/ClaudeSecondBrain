# Chapter 4 — The Apartment

David Lang returned to his apartment after the rehearsal and the neurological event involving the 68.48 Hz frequency. The narrative establishes the immediate aftermath of the manifestation and his father Arthur's recent death.

## Narrative Events

David's lecture binder, which he prepared before Arthur's funeral, was altered. The pages contained no lecture notes. Instead, they featured hand-drawn spirals (contracting, expanding, doubled, and reversed) and a crude sketch of a tuning fork surrounded by nested squares (source: string-theory-chapter-04-working-tab-check.md). 

A temporal anomaly occurred with a rocks glass. David poured scotch, placed the glass on the counter, and walked away. The glass shattered on the counter approximately fifteen seconds after he released it (source: string-theory-chapter-04-working-tab-check.md). 

David attempted to call Arthur's phone. The call routed to Arthur's voicemail. David spoke into the receiver but did not leave a substantive message. He then observed his reflection in the mantel mirror, noting the emergence of his father's features in his own face. When he touched the mirror, the reflection temporarily divided, layering Arthur's face beneath his own (source: string-theory-chapter-04-working-tab-check.md).

David pulled down his counterweighted chalkboard and attempted to calculate the parameters of the day's events. He recorded the frequency 68.48 Hz and the interval ratio 3:2. He listed three time durations: fifteen minutes and thirteen seconds; five minutes; and three minutes and forty-seven seconds. He stopped before writing "twenty years," a reference to a memory of Arthur critiquing David's first solo recital (source: string-theory-chapter-04-working-tab-check.md).
