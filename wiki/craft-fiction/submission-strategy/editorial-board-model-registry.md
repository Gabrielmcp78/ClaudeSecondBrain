# Editorial Board Model Registry

*Created: 2026-07-14*

## Status

This registry records the five diagnostic editor-models used for high-level evaluation of *String Theory* and comparable upmarket literary speculative projects. These are simulation lenses, not impersonations or claims of private editorial opinion.

The governing protocol is [Editorial Board Simulation Protocol](editorial-board-simulation-protocol.md). Research must follow [Editor and Agent Research Standard](editor-agent-research-standard.md).

## 1. Lee Boudreaux / Doubleday Lineage Model

**Simulation function:** Tests emotional inevitability, moral tenderness, literary scale, and whether the speculative premise earns its largest gestures through human consequence.

**Questions this model should ask:**

- Is the novel's emotional logic stronger than its intellectual display?
- Does grief alter choices, relationships, and irreversible outcomes?
- Does the ending transform the reader's understanding of the human story?
- Can the book support a major-house argument based on literary ambition and broad emotional reach?

**Reliability status:** Public-evidence profile requires current-role verification and a documented bibliography before claims about personal taste are used. Until then, treat all stated preferences as **S — simulation construct** or **I2 — tentative inference**.

## 2. Sarah McGrath / Riverhead Lineage Model

**Simulation function:** Tests protagonist magnetism, relational heat, cultural immediacy, and sustained reader attachment.

**Questions this model should ask:**

- Does David generate fascination before the premise fully arrives?
- Do Celeste, Rune, family, students, and colleagues exert pressure rather than orbit him functionally?
- Is the protagonist's intelligence socially alive on the page?
- Does the novel create a readership conversation beyond admiration of craft?

**Reliability status:** Public-evidence profile requires current-role verification, recent title credits, and source-supported taste statements. Until completed, the lens remains primarily **S — simulation construct**.

## 3. Sean McDonald / MCD-FSG Lineage Model

**Simulation function:** Tests formal invention, aesthetic system, risk, genre permeability, and whether music, physics, structure, and typography alter the reader's experience.

**Current-status warning:** Public reporting indicates MCD/FSG closed in April 2026. This model must therefore be described as an **MCD-FSG lineage model**, never as a current MCD acquisitions seat, unless later evidence establishes a new role or institutional context.

**Questions this model should ask:**

- Has the novel invented a form demanded by its subject?
- Does musical architecture create narrative consequence?
- Are equations, frequencies, historical sequences, and temporal disruptions integrated into one aesthetic logic?
- Is the book taking a necessary risk, or arranging familiar prestige signals?

**Reliability status:** The broad formal-experimentation association has public support through the historical mandate of MCD/FSG and McDonald's published list. Specific manuscript reactions remain **S — simulation construct**.

## 4. Brian Etling / Knopf Lineage Model

**Simulation function:** Tests the bridge between literary fiction and speculative fiction, conceptual rigor, science/music authority, reader accessibility, and category credibility.

**Questions this model should ask:**

- Can an intelligent general reader follow the scientific stakes without dilution?
- Does specialized knowledge create drama rather than exposition?
- Are the speculative rules coherent enough to support emotional belief?
- Can agents, booksellers, reviewers, and sales representatives describe the novel in one persuasive frame?

**Reliability status:** Current role, public bibliography, and explicit taste evidence require verification. Until then, use the model as **S — simulation construct**, with any inferred Knopf-lineage values kept separate from claims about the individual.

## 5. Deborah Ghim / Ecco Lineage Model

**Simulation function:** Tests voice, place, atmosphere, professional subculture, social texture, and whether the novel's Cornell, music, academic, and scientific worlds feel inhabited.

**Questions this model should ask:**

- Does Cornell behave like an institution with history, hierarchy, embarrassment, and appetite?
- Do music and physics communities possess different social languages?
- Does atmosphere arise from material life and behavior rather than ornamental description?
- Can secondary characters appear to continue living beyond David's field of attention?

**Reliability status:** Current role and documented editorial record require verification. Ecco's broad literary identity cannot substitute for person-specific evidence. Treat this lens as **S — simulation construct** until the profile is researched.

## Required Research Backlog

For each editor-model, create a sourced profile containing current role, verification date, selected books, public interviews, repeated catalog patterns, agent relationships, and confidence labels.

Priority order:

1. Verify all five current roles.
2. Identify at least five documented titles for each editor where editorial credit is reliable.
3. Locate public interviews or panels that reveal editorial practice.
4. Identify recent agent relationships through reported deals.
5. Retire, revise, or relabel any model whose public record fails to support the diagnostic lens.

## Use with *String Theory*

The board should evaluate actual pages and the current canonical manuscript version. It must not rely on summaries when pages are available. For each criticism, quote or identify a precise scene, sequence, or recurring pattern. The board may use the project canon to check continuity, but manuscript evidence controls judgments about execution.

The five models should remain differently weighted:

- Boudreaux lineage: emotional and moral scale.
- McGrath lineage: character and relationship conversion.
- McDonald lineage: formal and aesthetic necessity.
- Etling lineage: conceptual and category credibility.
- Ghim lineage: voice, place, and inhabited world.

A strong board result is not consensus. It is a map of which publishing proposition the manuscript can presently sustain and which revision would change that proposition most.
