# Editor and Agent Research Standard

*Created: 2026-07-14*

## Purpose

This file defines the evidence standard for editor-model simulations, agent routing, submission strategy, and market guidance. It exists to prevent confident but fictional publishing advice.

## Source Hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Official publisher, imprint, agency, or author pages.
2. Publicly announced deals and personnel changes from reputable trade publications.
3. Recorded interviews, festival conversations, podcasts, panels, and editor-authored essays.
4. A documented list of books edited or represented.
5. Manuscript Wish List pages, QueryManager profiles, and agency submission pages.
6. Reputable secondary reporting.
7. Inference from catalog pattern.

Social posts may establish current interests or open/closed status but should not carry a major taste claim without corroboration.

## Evidence Labels

Every profile claim should carry one of these internal labels:

- **V1 — Verified current fact:** current role, current agency, current submission status, official biography.
- **V2 — Verified historical fact:** prior role, published title, announced deal, public interview.
- **I1 — Strong inference:** repeated pattern across several books or statements.
- **I2 — Tentative inference:** plausible reading of limited public evidence.
- **S — Simulation construct:** a deliberate lens used to test a manuscript, not a claim about the real person.

The user-facing answer need not display every label, but the research file must preserve the distinction.

## Profile Schema

Each named editor or agent profile should contain:

- Name
- Current role and organization
- Verification date
- Current-status source
- Selected publicly documented books or clients
- Public statements about taste or editorial practice
- Repeated catalog patterns
- Category strengths
- Apparent aversions or limits, only when publicly supported
- Relevance to the manuscript
- Confidence level
- Known agent/editor deal relationships
- Staleness warning date

## Staleness Rules

- Current role: reverify within 30 days of active submission planning.
- Open/closed query status: verify on the day of submission.
- Manuscript wish list: reverify within 14 days.
- Imprint mandate: reverify within 90 days.
- Catalog pattern: review the most recent 3–5 years where possible.
- Agent-editor route: prefer deals from the most recent 5 years, while older repeated relationships may remain relevant.

## Agent-to-Editor Route Standard

A specific route may be described as:

- **Documented route:** at least one publicly reported deal between the agent and editor.
- **Repeated route:** multiple publicly reported deals or a clearly recurring working relationship.
- **Taste-aligned route:** no documented deal found, but the agent's list and editor's catalog overlap meaningfully.
- **Speculative route:** plausible in theory, with insufficient public evidence. Avoid recommending this route as a primary strategy.

Never use “feeds,” “has access to,” “will get read by,” or “can sell to” without evidence.

## Manuscript-to-Editor Fit Test

A credible fit requires more than resemblance to one title. Evaluate:

- Sentence-level register
- Narrative scale
- Emotional engine
- Formal ambition
- Speculative density
- Reader accessibility
- Category and format
- Author profile
- Likely editorial workload
- The editor's recent list balance

A strong thematic match with a weak execution or category match remains a weak acquisition proposition.

## Probability Integrity

Acquisition probability is a simulation range, not a statistical forecast. It must never imply knowledge of:

- The editor's current list openings
- Budget
- P&L thresholds
- Publisher approval
- Sales forecasts
- Competitive submissions
- Internal house politics
- Personal enthusiasm

State the conditions that would move the estimate up or down.

## Research Maintenance Workflow

Before a major submission simulation:

1. Verify each named editor's current role.
2. Verify each named agent's current role and query status.
3. Review recent books, deals, or interviews.
4. Update the relevant profile file with sources and date.
5. Mark unsupported legacy claims for deletion or downgrade.
6. Run the simulation only after separating facts from inferred taste lenses.

## Known Reliability Warnings

- Publisher and agency staffing changes quickly.
- Imprints close, merge, or change mandate.
- Public book credits often fail to identify the acquiring editor.
- A house's brand does not define every editor's taste.
- A successful comparable title does not prove appetite for another similar manuscript.
- Agent websites and third-party databases frequently conflict.
- Private deal databases may contain information unavailable for independent verification.

## Output Language

Preferred:

- “The documented catalog suggests…”
- “This is a strong inference from…”
- “The route is plausible because…”
- “Current public evidence is insufficient to claim…”
- “The model uses this editor's lineage as a diagnostic lens.”

Avoid:

- “She will love this.”
- “He always buys books like this.”
- “This agent can place it there.”
- “The house is looking for…” without current sourcing.
- “The acquisition probability is 63%” without a range and conditions.
