# Query Letter — *String Theory*

## The Sales Engine (The Breakthrough Realization)

The query letter is not about:
- A solar storm
- A mysterious frequency
- Project ARGO
- Strange anomalies
- A scientific conspiracy
- A buried research capsule

Those are plot mechanics. They belong in the novel, not in the pitch.

The actual sales engine is:

**A physicist who built his life on a twenty-year act of silence is forced to confront a phenomenon tied to his family lineage, his dead father, and the one truth he has spent his life refusing to hear.**

Once the query is organized around that spine, all the ARGO material, the resonance mechanics, the frequency, the capsule, the solar storm, and Celeste become supporting evidence rather than premise. The query that failed consistently was thriller-shaped: event-driven, summary-driven, mechanics-forward. The query that works is character-driven: a man, a bargain, a lineage, a reckoning.

## The Final Query Letter

---

Dear [Agent Name],

David Lang spent twenty years making sure the world would never have to hear what he'd discovered. He almost succeeded.

As a physicist at Cornell University, David has spent his life studying the hidden structures that connect seemingly unrelated things. He believes every system can be understood if you listen carefully enough. But after the death of his father — the one relationship he never managed to understand — he is pulled back toward a mystery he buried two decades ago and swore never to revisit.

Twenty years earlier, David and Celeste Armitage, his closest collaborator and the partner he has spent two decades avoiding, uncovered evidence suggesting that resonance is more than a property of sound. The implications were profound enough that the work was buried, the records sealed, and the participants persuaded to walk away. David accepted the bargain. He built a prestigious academic career on top of that silence.

Now a frequency he cannot escape surfaces throughout his life — in lecture halls, in abandoned equations, in systems that should not contain it. The closer he moves toward the truth, the harder it becomes to distinguish discovery from participation. What initially appears to be a forgotten scientific anomaly reveals itself as something older: a phenomenon stretching backward through his father, his grandfather, and a chain of unfinished choices that long predate David's involvement.

Throughout his life, David dismissed his father's insistence that some things are not meant for an audience. Arthur abandoned the pursuit of explanation and found refuge in music instead. David chose science. Now, as the boundaries between the two begin to collapse, David must confront the possibility that his father understood something essential he never did — and that to face the truth, he must also face Celeste, and the relationship that has quietly shaped and damaged his life for twenty years.

As the phenomenon accelerates, David faces a choice. He can continue treating the resonance as a problem to solve, preserving the scientific identity that has defined his life, or he can surrender to the transmission his father spent decades trying to teach him to hear. Either way, the life he built upon silence cannot survive the truth.

STRING THEORY is an upmarket literary speculative novel complete at 96,000 words. At its heart, it is a novel about mishearing — the people we love, the stories we inherit, and the signals we spend a lifetime trying to force into coherence. It will appeal to readers of Benjamin Labatut's MANIAC and Scott Alexander Howard's *The Other Valley*.

I am a composer, director, educator, and arts leader with more than thirty years of professional experience.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Gabriel McPherson

## Three Simulation-Derived Fixes (Applied 2026-06-06)

The following changes were made to the letter based on elite literary agent simulation review:

1. **Cut "Now impossible things are happening."** — The paragraph now opens cold: "Now a frequency he cannot escape..." The original opener was too on-the-nose and genre-signaling.
2. **Cut "The deeper he digs, the harder it becomes to tell whether he is investigating the phenomenon or simply fulfilling it."** — This line was redundant with "distinguish discovery from participation" in the same paragraph. One of the two; not both.
3. **Compressed the generational block.** "Gradually reveals itself as something older and more personal: a phenomenon stretching backward through generations of his family, connecting his father, his grandfather, and a chain of unfinished choices" → "reveals itself as something older: a phenomenon stretching backward through his father, his grandfather, and a chain of unfinished choices." Tighter, less padded.

## Structural Analysis

### The One-Line Hook

"David Lang spent twenty years making sure the world would never have to hear what he'd discovered. He almost succeeded."

The hook works because "He almost succeeded" implies the global-scale event (the solar storm, the worldwide broadcast) without describing or naming it. Agents feel the weight of the stakes without the query pivoting toward thriller territory. "The world would never have to hear" also plants the novel's central irony — a man defined by the act of listening who has spent his career preventing others from doing the same.

The hook was specifically designed to answer a structural problem: the novel has worldwide stakes that differentiate it commercially, but naming those stakes (solar storm, billions watching) makes the query read as high-concept thriller rather than literary speculative fiction. The solution is to gesture at scale from inside David's psychology.

### The Through-Line

"Every system can be understood if you listen carefully enough" (paragraph one) echoes in "the transmission his father spent decades trying to teach him to hear" (penultimate paragraph). The irony is architectural: the man who believes everything yields to careful listening is the one who spent twenty years refusing to listen. This does not need to be stated — it is built into the query's structure and agents who are reading carefully will feel it.

### The Thematic Noun

"Mishearing" is the precise word for what the novel is about. It does triple duty: acoustic (the frequency, the transmission), interpersonal (David and Arthur, David and Celeste), and epistemological (the difference between scientific proof and genuine understanding). Earlier query drafts used "longing to be truly heard" — accurate but passive. "Mishearing" is active, specific, and unexpected enough to be memorable.

### Celeste

In early drafts, Celeste appeared as a ghost — named, but without definition. The fix: introduce her in the buried-history paragraph as "the partner he has spent two decades avoiding," then return to her in the Arthur section as the second confrontation David cannot escape. Her weight as a character comes from the pairing: to face the truth, David must face both his father's legacy and the person he has been avoiding since the bargain was made.

### Comps

*MANIAC* (Benjamin Labatut, 2023) — the closest structural precedent: literary speculative fiction that uses a scientist's obsessive pursuit of underlying order to examine the cost of that pursuit at a human scale. Demonstrates the register: not genre science fiction, not thriller. The comp is within the 3-year rule.

*The Other Valley* (Scott Alexander Howard, 2024) — quiet literary speculative fiction, grief-centered, Booker Prize longlist. Establishes that the novel is literary fiction with a speculative premise, not genre speculative fiction. Within the 3-year rule.

**Rejected comp:** *The Time of Our Singing* (Richard Powers, 2003) — 23 years old, violates the 3-year mid-list rule. Despite being the closest thematic template (music, family, inheritance, time across generations), it cannot appear in the query.

## Query Evolution — What Was Rejected and Why

Earlier drafts that failed consistently shared one of four problems:

**Too thriller-shaped.** Queries that opened with the solar storm, the global broadcast, or the Cornell conspiracy framed the novel as a high-concept event thriller. That frame is accurate to the plot but wrong for the pitch — it sets agent expectations the literary execution cannot satisfy.

**Too explanatory.** Queries that explained the 68.48 Hz mechanic, Project ARGO, or the resonance science spent 80 words on mechanics that belong in the novel. The frequency should appear in the query as a felt presence, not a described phenomenon.

**Too vague.** Queries that stayed entirely in emotional abstraction ("a man confronting his past") gave agents nothing to hold. The Cornell bargain, the buried research, the multigenerational lineage — these are the specific facts that make the abstraction land.

**Summary-driven.** Queries that moved chronologically through plot events produced a synopsis rather than a sales pitch. The query is not a summary. It is an argument for why this novel exists and why this agent should want to read it.
