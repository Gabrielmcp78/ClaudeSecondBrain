# Speculative Fiction Craft Principles

*Gabriel McPherson's speculative fiction craft is built upon formal structural frameworks, precise scene-level tempo control, and the positive-declaration rule, transforming speculative science into structured narrative arcs.* (source: CLAUDE.md, themes-and-canon.md)

---

## 1. The Positive-Declaration Rule

In speculative and hard-science fiction, world-building mechanics and speculative phenomena must be declared with positive, concrete, and physical properties rather than negative phrasing or mystical ambiguity:
- **Avoid Vague Negations**: Prose should not define a speculative element by what it is *not* (e.g., "The frequency was not a normal sound, and no one knew where it came from").
- **Declare Concrete Physicality**: The writer declares the rules as standing, objective physical laws within the universe (e.g., "The 68.48 Hz carrier wave entrains biological theta brainwave oscillations via electromagnetic coupling").
- **Narrative Consequence**: Grounding the Speculative Reality in positive declarations makes the fictional science feel self-consistent and non-arbitrary, allowing the reader to accept the cosmic stakes as absolute facts.

---

## 2. Structural Frameworks: Musical Form

The architecture of the novel mirrors musical composition, using classical structures to dictate narrative counterpoint and pacing:
- **Musical Subtitles**: Individual chapters are subtitled and organized around specific musical forms (e.g., *Epistemic fugue in four voices*, *Secular passion in eight movements*, *Passacaglia with chorale*).
- **Voice Counterpoint**: In a fugue chapter (e.g., Chapter 2), different timelines or character perspectives act as musical voices pursuing the same thematic subject, entering and exiting according to contrapuntal rules.
- **Form Constraints**: Structural forms are not decorative; they enforce rigorous constraints on character count, scene lengths, and dialogue transitions, translating musical physics into narrative progression.

---

## 3. Scene-Level Craft: Tempo and Dynamics

Scenes are composed using concepts drawn directly from musical notation to govern dramatic tension and reader experience:
- **Tempo Markings**: Narrative pacing is structured around velocity markings (*Andante*, *Presto*, *Largo*). Action scenes employ short, fragmented sentence structures (staccato) to accelerate pace, while reflective passages utilize flowing, complex sentences (legato).
- **Tension & Dissonance**: Dramatic tension is built by introducing narrative dissonance (conflicting agendas, mismatched dialogue) and slowly resolving it toward thematic harmony.
- **Psychic Distance (Dynamics)**: The narrative voice expands and contracts its psychic distance dynamically—shifting from deep internal stream-of-consciousness (close-up) to detached, archival overview (wide shot)—matching the emotional volume (crescendo and decrescendo) of the scene's stakes.
