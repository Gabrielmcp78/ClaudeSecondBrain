## Chapter 10: Fractional Currency
**Words:** 3,822 | **Scenes:** 5
**Setting:** November 28, 2026 — David's birthday; the convergence date

David encounters a former student (Thornden) who appears translucent and unresponsive. A clementine refuses to roll into a storm drain. He experiences temporal displacement: 53 unaccounted-for minutes.

Traffic stops. David detects an anomalous frequency of 86.84 Hz, which descends to 68.48 Hz as he approaches his apartment. A disembodied voice: "Symmetry attracts violence. Listen."

His mother calls on his birthday; they discuss his father's Sunday morning cello performances. She encourages him to play.

David reveals a hidden slate wall covered with physics equations and storm forecasts — an elaborate experiment designed to exploit the solar storm's resonant frequencies.

The experiment fails. He discovers a handwritten note from his father Arthur Lang containing a Bach score with the inscription: "Don't judge it, my son. Just once, for me, just play."

## Chapter 11: The Symmetry of Violence
**Subtitle:** fantasia on Bach's Sarabande in four movements | **Words:** 3,532 | **Movements:** 4

David plays Bach's Sarabande. As he plays, a solar storm triggers cascading transformer failures: 744 transformers fail sequentially across upstate New York, then nearly 200,000 worldwide.

David's emotional release synchronizes with the electromagnetic storm's escalation — the connection between his inner turbulence and the external event is not metaphorical.

A counterweight cable fails; his chalkboard wall collapses; chalk dust engulfs the apartment. David appears to upload encrypted data before losing consciousness. His fate is left ambiguous — death or transcendence.
