## Plot

Celeste discovers something amiss in her home after a restless night. David arrives at rehearsal visibly injured from the Chapter 3 assault. Linn finds him in his apartment and provides medical care without interrogation.

The quartet performs Bach's Sarabande in a chapel, where 68.48 Hz directly interferes with their music. David proposes an experimental tuning using a syntonic comma interval; the group achieves unexpected harmonic alignment.

Walking scenes (the "interstitials") intersperse dramatic moments — Linn and David share memories of childhood mishaps, deepening their bond. David suffers a panic attack on campus triggered by an ember and ambient vibration. Celeste comforts him; they walk to her house on the hill.

At Celeste's home, David rests and works on his scientific paper while she composes nearby. She offers presence without assumption. David returns to his apartment alone.

Celeste repairs the broken bannister finial herself — asserting independence and ownership. The house "settles" into acceptance.

## Key Elements

**68.48 Hz in the chapel:** The frequency directly interferes with the quartet rehearsal — first time it enters the musical space as an active force rather than a private sensation.

**Syntonic comma tuning:** David's proposed interval adjustment achieves unexpected harmonic alignment, suggesting the experimental framework from Chapter 3 (Pythagorean tuning / Celeste's modified harmonic intervals) is productive.

**Scene tempo markings:** Each of the 14 scenes carries a musical tempo marking (Largo, Andante, Adagio, etc.), making the chapter itself a multi-movement composition. The "interstitials" function as transitional passages between dramatic scenes.

**The house on the hill:** Celeste's home has agency, memory, and mood — it responds to presence and absence. Functions as a character.

**Panic attack:** First explicit physiological crisis; triggered by an ember and ambient vibration. Celeste is the one who reaches him.
