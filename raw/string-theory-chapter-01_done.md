## Movement I: The Ivy League

David Lang, physicist and holder of the Feynman Chair of Quantum Dynamics at Cornell, returns to campus after his father's funeral. Exhausted (three energy drinks, 400 miles of interstate), he arrives nearly late and discovers his watch has lost five minutes inexplicably. He experiences a low-frequency buzz he somehow knows to be 68.48 Hz.

During his quantum dynamics lecture David drifts mentally, writes unusual noise terms into equations, and grows agitated when Dr. Rune Ishikawa (department chair) interrupts with pointed questions about harmony versus resonance. A Polaroid mysteriously appears in his lecture slides — a tuning fork buried in soil, taken years ago with Celeste Armitage and Marcus during the burial of a time capsule containing their graduate-school manifestos on acoustic physics.

The lecture ends with David nearly catatonic at the blackboard. A student breaks his fugue with humor. He dismisses class, falls asleep in the empty hall.

Walking through the quad, David is drawn to the bench above the buried capsule. The frequency returns — overwhelming and directional, emanating from beneath the ground. He screams; the sound shakes windows and stone.

## Movement II: The Office

David's office holds decades of abandoned work: rejected papers, failed grants, a cello case gathering dust. Dr. Rune Ishikawa is waiting. They discuss a Princeton University Press monograph about David's decoherence research. Rune is oblique — praising old work while implying current output lacks speculative rigor. Final provocation: "Resonance equals survival?" He departs with a knowing smile.

David is left with his father's unexecuted will. He cannot bring himself to finalize his father's death through legal machinery.

## Movement III: The Rehearsal Hall

The quartet rehearses Prokofiev's String Quartet No. 2 ("Kabardinian"). David's playing is technically flawless but emotionally absent — mind lost in harmonic analysis and standing wave patterns visible in dust motes.

Celeste Armitage (violist, David's former partner) pleads: "David… stay with us. Stay with me." The words carry the weight of years of him evaporating into abstraction.

During the third movement's col legno section, David's cello C-string snaps with a gunshot crack. Roger Linn and Eleanor Rieger excuse themselves tactfully, leaving David and Celeste alone. A moment of genuine connection; she departs into the snow-lit quad.

## Movement IV: The Apartment

David's high-ceilinged loft: spiral staircase with glass treads, three counterweighted chalkboard panels, sheet music everywhere. He attempts to eat, writes in his notebook, finds his father's number in his phone favorites, calls it. His father's answering machine greeting plays. The impossibility of the moment crushes him.

Looking in the mirror, David sees his father's face overlaying his own. He contemplates the liminal space between tremolo and theorem where his best work exists, forever fleeing observation.

By 2:47 a.m., David stands equidistant from stove, chalkboard, and cello case — three vertices of unresolved potential. Outside, the earth sounds its eternal B-flat.

## Characters

**David Lang** — Physicist; Feynman Chair of Quantum Dynamics at Cornell. Recently bereaved (father Arthur Lang died two weeks prior). Former partner of Celeste Armitage. Brilliant but increasingly unstable. Experiencing the 68.48 Hz phenomenon. Divides attention between theoretical physics and chamber music.

**Celeste Armitage** — Violist; David's ex-partner of 20+ years (never married). Observant, emotionally direct, grounded. Senses David's dissociation. Represents the human, relational world David abandons for equations.

**Dr. Rune Ishikawa** — Department chair; guardian of East Hill. Elegant, manipulative, strategically placed. Questions David's current work; implies decline from earlier brilliance. Language use: surgical precision.

**Roger Linn** — First violinist; Professor of String Performance. David's friend for 30+ years. Tactful, protective of the quartet's equilibrium.

**Eleanor Rieger** — Second violinist; diplomat-like warmth. Offers condolences about Arthur Lang.

**Arthur Lang** (deceased) — David's father; former second chair with BSO. Wanted music for David, not physics. Haunts David through inherited features and unfinished conversation.

**Marcus** (mentioned) — Third member of the capsule burial. Identity otherwise undetailed.

**Ralph** — Custodian; philosopher-janitor who cleans Feynman Hall at night. Offers David wisdom about resonance and survival.

## Key Symbols & Mechanics

**68.48 Hz:** Arrives unbidden in David's consciousness. The fundamental resonance of the buried capsule. "Almost half the fine-structure." Scratches at skull, vibrates molars, pulses through sternum. Grows louder when David approaches the burial site.

**The Capsule:** Graduate-school manifestos sealed ~20 years ago beneath the bench. Sealed with agreement: "if it's real, it'll claw its way out."

**The Tuning Fork Polaroid:** Represents the capsule burial moment. Mysteriously appears in David's lecture slides. Threshold symbol: who they were vs. who they became.

**The Chalkboard:** Counterweighted, dormant, threatening. Represents thought and erasure. Final equation written: RESONANCE = SURVIVAL.

**Time Discrepancies:** David's watch runs behind. Minutes disappear. Clock readings don't align with experienced duration.

**The Cello C-string snap:** Break in the music; break in control. The road not fully taken.
